Unykorn Revenue Architecture · Toll-Booth Economics

Deterministic payment and compliance processing for real-world assets. Fees on every capital movement. Zero balance-sheet risk. Zero broker-dealer threshold exposure.

Unykorn LLC does not custody assets, hold investor capital, or take transaction-based securities compensation. It operates the compliance and attestation rails through which every dollar flowing across the four-entity stack must pass. Each pass triggers a named, deterministic fee — extracted through the on-chain FeeCollector contract with pull-and-immediate-forward routing to the Unykorn treasury.

The economic frame

Think of Unykorn as a payment and compliance processor for tokenized real-world assets. Every event that a securities counsel, senior lender, or CPA administrator would want documented — subscription funds moving into escrow, identity verification, PPA assignment attestation, EPC milestone certification, waterfall distribution — passes through a Unykorn contract. That contract emits an event, hashes the evidence, and (where the fee schedule applies) routes a basis-point or fixed fee to the treasury.

This is not novel financially. It is the same model that Visa, Mastercard, and Stripe use in the payment-processing industry: pass-through infrastructure that captures a small fraction of every transaction while never taking custody of the underlying value. What is novel is applying it to the RWA layer where the transactions are securities issuance, distribution, compliance-gated transfer, and attestation-anchored draws.

The regulatory geometry Because Unykorn is not a broker-dealer, not a custodian, and not the issuer of any security, its fees are technology fees. Setup, monthly SaaS, per-attestation, per-distribution, issuance basis points on primary mints (paid by the SPE, not by the investor), and license fees are all technology-service fees under standard commercial-services accounting. There is no securities compensation, no custody licensing, no MTL exposure. The regulatory footprint is trivial relative to the revenue scale.

Zero balance sheet — how the toll booth stays clean

  1. No custody · Subscription funds go to bank / trust escrow. Distributions go from Site SPE to token holder wallets or to BitGo Bank & Trust. Nothing ever touches a Unykorn wallet or bank account.
  2. No securities compensation · Unykorn does not sell securities, does not refer investors for compensation, does not paper subscriptions. All securities work runs through LD Capital (issuer) and FTH Trading (distributor with BD-of-record / own BD).
  3. Pull-and-immediate-forward fee routing · The on-chain FeeCollector contract collects fee events but does not retain balance. Each fee is pulled from the payer (Site SPE) and immediately forwarded to the Unykorn treasury multisig. Zero float between transferFrom and transfer means zero custody exposure.
  4. Non-transaction-based comp until BD-of-record executes · Prior to FTH Trading's BD-of-record engagement, no compensation flows to Unykorn or FTH that could be construed as securities compensation. Post-engagement, distribution fees flow to FTH under BD engagement letter, still not to Unykorn.
  5. Named counterparty every step · Senior lenders interact only with the Site SPE. QROF fund investors interact only with LD Capital as fund administrator. Custody interacts only with BitGo. Unykorn is invisible to counterparties who don't need to know about the rails.

Why external sponsors license the stack (Track-A)

The economic case for an outside QROF operator (a Redivider-adjacent group, other data-center sponsors, hospitality tokenization groups) to license Unykorn's rails rather than build custom compliance code is straightforward:

  • Time-to-market — Unykorn's stack is ~$3-5M and 18-24 months of engineering to replicate from scratch, with dual audits before any live value. Licensing costs $150-300k setup + $10-25k/mo.
  • Regulatory safety — The 4-eyes flow, IdentityRegistry, and travel-rule gate are already audit-tested. Building custom risks compliance mistakes that the SEC or FINRA will find later.
  • Interoperability — Licensed SPEs can plug into partner ATSs and Centrifuge V3 pools that recognize ERC-3643 with the specific compliance modules Unykorn has deployed. Custom implementations don't get that recognition.
  • Focus — External sponsors are experts in their asset class (hospitality, mining, healthcare real estate). They aren't smart-contract firms and don't want to be. Licensing lets them focus on origination and deal-making.

Master Services Agreement (MSA) structure

Each SPE that uses the rails signs an MSA with Unykorn LLC covering:

  • Setup fee (one-time) and payment terms
  • Monthly SaaS fee and payment cadence
  • Per-attestation and per-distribution fee schedule
  • Issuance bps schedule at primary mint
  • Service levels (uptime, response time to attestation requests, incident response)
  • Data ownership and portability (SPE owns its data)
  • Term and termination (12-month initial, then month-to-month)
  • License scope (SPE's specific deal, not resellable without separate license)
The Master Services Agreement is technology-services, not securities The MSA is a commercial-services contract like any Stripe, AWS, or Twilio agreement. It is not a securities placement engagement, not a custody agreement, and not a broker-dealer engagement letter. Securities counsel reviews the MSA to confirm it does not inadvertently create securities-compensation exposure.

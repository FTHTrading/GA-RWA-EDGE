1 · Executive summary

GA-RWA-EDGE is an institutional infrastructure for issuing, servicing, and distributing tokenized ownership interests in Georgia-based edge data-center sites. The stack is optimized for existing operational sites with locked PPA power positions — the anchor case is a 5 MW Atlanta-market site currently hosting 721 bitcoin miners at 4.9 cents per kilowatt-hour under a fixed-rate power purchase agreement in a deregulated gas zone.

The stack separates four operating roles into four distinct legal entities. This separation is not stylistic. It is what allows senior lenders (typically CRE, USDA B&I, or C-PACE) to interface with a clean single-purpose entity that owes them senior debt, while securities regulators observe issuance under Regulation D Rule 506(c) with counsel-opined Rule 144 tacking language, while custodians hold assets in a bank-chartered qualified vault, and while the technology company earns fees without balance-sheet risk or FINRA licensing exposure.

The four entities are Unykorn LLC (technology rails), LD Capital LLC (issuance and Qualified Opportunity Fund administration), FTH Trading (securities distribution and secondary markets), and Asset SPEs (bankruptcy-remote borrowers holding physical assets and revenue contracts). Money never sits with Unykorn or FTH. Assets live only in Site SPEs.

One-line thesis Contracted AI hosting revenue backed by locked PPA power, wrapped in a Qualified Rural Opportunity Fund equity tranche, distributed via ERC-3643 permissioned securities on non-custodial rails with waterfall discipline that treats the utility payment as Priority 1.

2 · Investment thesis

Four factors intersect. Any two of them constitute a real deal. All four together are rare.

  1. Locked-in power at a below-market rate. The Barak site has a fixed-rate PPA at 4.9 cents per kilowatt-hour. Wholesale power in most metro Southeast markets currently runs 6–9 cents. Locked-in below-market power is the largest single input cost advantage in compute infrastructure.
  2. Existing operational site. This is not a greenfield build with a two-year interconnect queue and a three-year construction schedule. It is a running facility with 721 miners currently drawing power, revenue-generating today. Conversion is a retrofit, not a construction project.
  3. OZ 2.0 Qualified Rural Opportunity Fund tier (pending tract designation). If the site's census tract is designated rural under the 2027 OZ 2.0 map, cap-gains investors receive a 30% basis step-up at year 5 (vs. 10% for standard QOF) and a 50% substantial-improvement threshold. On $4M of QROF equity that means roughly $1.2M of federal tax benefit before any project-level appreciation is considered.
  4. Non-custodial rails with public code. The 27-contract smart-contract suite is open-source, audit-track, and compiling under solc 0.8.36 with EVM Cancun. Every distribution, every attestation, and every transfer runs through published contracts. There is no black box operational risk.

3.1 Why four entities and not one

Consolidation attracts problems from every side. A single-entity operator that holds assets, issues securities, custody funds, and operates rails needs (a) a broker-dealer registration for any transaction-based securities compensation, (b) qualified-custodian licensing for held customer assets, (c) SPE-clean separation for every senior debt facility, and (d) a compliance calendar spanning FINRA, SEC, state MTL, and any tokenization-specific rules. The regulatory footprint is enormous and the audit fees compound.

Four entities with disciplined role separation solves each problem in its natural place: senior lenders sign against Site SPEs, custody sits at BitGo Bank & Trust N.A. or equivalent OCC-chartered institution, securities distribution runs through FTH Trading (BD-of-record in Stage 1, own BD Stage 2, LDX-as-ATS Stage 3), and Unykorn earns technology fees only — never transaction-based securities compensation.

4.1 ReserveProofAnchor — the evidence chain

Every material real-world fact underlying a deal is hashed and anchored on-chain via ReserveProofAnchor. This includes the executed PPA document, EPC scope + FAT/SAT/IST certificates, hosting-contract counterparty and terms, appraisals, zoning verification letters, utility load letters, SREC registry serials, and gas-generation emissions attestations. The anchor produces a permanent, auditable proof that the SPE controls the asset and that the underwriting facts existed at claimed time.

The contract does not constitute legal ownership by itself. It evidences that a specific document existed at a specific time with a specific hash, signed by an m-of-n oracle quorum. Legal collateralization (the SPV structure, security agreement, control agreement, and counsel opinion) lives off-chain and is referenced by legalRef.

4.2 CMBSWaterfall — deterministic distribution

Distributions execute deterministically. Priority 1 is always the utility / PPA payment. Priority 2 is senior debt service. Priority 3 is platform fees (Unykorn + LD Capital). Priority 4 is tax-equity allocations if applicable. Priority 5 is subordinate / QROF equity. Priority 6 is sponsor promote.

Priority 1 is Priority 1 because a missed utility payment triggers PPA termination, which collapses the deal thesis. The waterfall math tests DSCR net of PPA demand charges — not gross of them. This is the single most important underwriting distinction between a compute-hosting deal and a traditional real-estate deal.

5 · Compliance framework

The tokenized security is an ERC-3643 permissioned token. Every transfer runs through IdentityRegistry (which maps wallet to verified identity + accreditation status + country) and AMLTravelRuleGate (which enforces travel-rule data payload for over-threshold transfers). Modular compliance additionally supports Rule144LockupModule (holding-period enforcement per counsel opinion), CountryRestrictModule (jurisdictional gating for Reg S), and MaxHoldersModule (Reg D 506(c) beneficial-holder count cap).

Compliance is a hard gate — value cannot move unless the compliance check passes. An unverified wallet is structurally unable to receive tokens. This is enforcement before movement, not review after.

7 · Tax framework

7.1 QROF (Qualified Rural Opportunity Fund) — the primary tax lever

A cap-gains investor with $4M in eligible gains rolls the gains into the QROF within 180 days. The gains are deferred. After a continuous 5-year hold, the investor receives a 30% basis step-up (versus 10% for standard QOF). On $4M of deferred gain, that is $1.2M of basis increase — worth roughly $286k in federal tax savings at the 23.8% LTCG + NIIT rate.

After a 10-year hold, appreciation on the QROF interest itself is generally excluded from tax (basis reset to fair market value on sale, subject to OZ 2.0 30-year FMV election rules). This is separate from the 5-year step-up.

7.2 MACRS + 100% bonus depreciation

The 100% bonus depreciation restored permanently under OBBBA applies to non-solar equipment placed in service after January 19, 2025. This includes IT equipment (5-year), immersion cooling and racks (5-year), UPS and switchgear (5- or 7-year), and land improvements (15-year). No solar ITC basis reduction applies because no solar ITC is being claimed (see 7.4).

On the Barak site, roughly $13M of the $20M project cost qualifies as 5-year MACRS property with 100% bonus. That produces a Year-1 deduction of $13M against ordinary income for the taking partner — most valuable to a partnership-flip tax-equity partner if one is retained, or to the sponsor otherwise.

7.3 Section 179

The 2026 §179 limit is $2.56M with phase-out beginning at $4.09M and fully phased out at $6.65M. On a Barak-scale project this is meaningful for early-stage equipment purchases before bonus depreciation kicks in on other property.

7.4 Solar / wind ITC §48E — cutoff missed

The begin-construction safe harbor for solar and wind ITC under Section 48E lapsed on July 4, 2026. New solar / wind builds no longer qualify. The Barak project accordingly does not model solar ITC into the base capital stack. Storage-only ITC survives through 2033 with begin-construction runway, and 45Q carbon capture credits remain available, but neither is modeled as a live layer in the base case.

12 · BD-of-record → own BD → LDX-as-ATS

FTH Trading follows a disciplined three-stage broker-dealer path. Stage 1 is a BD-of-record partnership: a registered broker-dealer papers third-party securities placement, FTH refers investors and handles all non-securities work. Stage 2 is FTH filing Form NMA to become its own FINRA-registered BD (12-18 months, $250-750k). Stage 3 is FTH's LDX venue filing Form ATS to operate as an Alternative Trading System for secondary trading.

Until Stage 2 closes, FTH takes no transaction-based securities compensation. Its 2026-27 revenue is (a) technology fees under the Unykorn / LD Capital service agreements and (b) the BD-of-record partnership economics. Skipping Stage 1 and taking finder fees on third-party paper is the classic unregistered-BD trap — SEC rescission rights kill cap tables.

13 · Risk factors

An investment in tokenized Site SPE interests involves substantial risk including the possible loss of the entire investment. Key risks include:

  • PPA counterparty risk: If the PPA is not cleanly assignable or the counterparty utility does not accept novation, the deal restructures around a different anchor asset.
  • Interconnect and utility risk: Sub-5 MW loads are ordinary commercial customers on standard tariffs, but any utility policy shift or tariff increase directly reduces DSCR.
  • Rural QROF designation risk: The 2027 QOZ map is not certified until Q4 2026. If the target tract is not designated as rural, the fund reverts to standard QOF (10% step-up).
  • Regulatory risk: OZ 2.0 rules, ERC-3643 recognition, Reg D interpretation, and BD-of-record economics all involve regulatory framework that may change.
  • Technology risk: The smart-contract suite is unaudited. Pre-mainnet blockers include dual independent audits before any value moves on chain.
  • Tenant concentration risk: Hosting contracts with a single AI-cloud counterparty concentrate credit risk. The stack encourages multi-tenant, multi-workload discipline.
  • Liquidity risk: Site SPE interests are illiquid. Secondary trading via partner ATS (Stage 1) or LDX (Stage 3) provides limited liquidity subject to Rule 144 holding periods.
  • Tax risk: QROF benefits depend on tract designation, hold period, and investor-specific facts. Not all cap-gains rollovers qualify. State tax conformity varies.

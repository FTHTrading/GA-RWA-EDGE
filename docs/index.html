What clients should look for — the 10 items that separate real platforms from marketing

  1. Show me the code — inspectable contracts, published addresses at deploy. Marketing sites describing nonexistent systems are the industry's #1 red flag.
  2. Who holds the keys — qualified custody, model named. Ours: 2-of-3 with the client holding the backup key; the platform can never move funds alone.
  3. Where money sits pre-close — bank/trust escrow only, never a platform operating account. Never send subscription funds to a "platform wallet."
  4. Explicit fees — every fee a named bps or dollar line. Opaque spreads are unauditable fees.
  5. No promised returns — guaranteed yield, SBLC "monetization," or upfront release fees = walk away, from anyone.
  6. Licenses verified — issuers may sell their own paper (506(c)); anyone selling others' securities for compensation must be a registered BD (check FINRA BrokerCheck).
  7. Registry-backed ESG — a REC or carbon credit without a tracking-registry serial is a story, not an asset. Our contracts refuse to mint without one.
  8. Compliance as a gate — an unverified wallet must be structurally unable to receive tokens. Enforcement before movement, not review after.
  9. Reproducible math — waterfall distributions reconcile to an independent model to the cent. Ask for the reconciliation any period.
  10. Engineered downside — DSCR triggers, cash traps, loss order, and key-recovery answers should be crisp. If they aren't, the downside was never designed.
